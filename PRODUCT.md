# Enamel: product one-pager

**What it is:** the issuer studio behind Sash. A parametric editor for badges and
certificates, with versions, publishing, and claim links that carry an expiry, a
seat limit and an allow list.

**Who it is for:** the person in a team who wants to award something to other
people and has no interest in opening a drawing tool. A tech lead marking the end
of a knowledge-transfer series, a guild running a challenge, somebody who wants to
give a colleague a Rubber Duck Whisperer badge that looks like an object rather
than an emoji.

**Register:** product (a tool someone uses), not brand (a page someone visits).
Two columns, dense controls, an accordion of parameters, and a preview that keeps
up with the typing.

**The scene:** a laptop on a Thursday afternoon, ten minutes before the retro,
with a name already in mind and no design skill claimed. The author picks the
nearest of 24 finished presets, changes the words, presses Publish, and pastes a
claim link into the channel.

**What done looks like:** a published template with a claim link in the clipboard,
and an issuer who can come back later and see who took a seat.

**What it deliberately refuses:** naming a real body as the issuer. The blocklist
runs inside the publish mutation and reads the name, the description, the criteria
and every word drawn on the artefact, and the provenance strip is rendered into
the pixels rather than into the page around them. Parody, not forgery, is a
property of what comes out of the exporter rather than a line in a footer.
